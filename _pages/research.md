## Research agenda
Whether political parties pay an electoral cost for moderation — and how electoral institutions condition that cost — across ideological (left-right) competition in Europe and ethnic politics in deeply divided societies.

## Publications
**Mass Attitudes towards Russia's Aggression against Ukraine: Tentative Support for Top-Down Opinion Formation.** *European Journal of Political Research* (2026, Open Access).
With Filip Kostelka, Martín Alberdi, Max Bradley, Toine Fiselier, Alexandra Jabbour, Eleonora Minaeva, Silvia Porciuleanu, and Diana Rafailova. [Read the paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1475676526101352)

## Working papers

**1. "Following the Demand: The Electoral Consequences of Party Shifts."**
29 European countries (Manifesto Project, CSES). Finds no general centrifugal advantage: parties, especially on the center-left, gain votes from position shifts that correct earlier moves away from their electorate's preferred position — regardless of direction.

**2. "Where Does Moderation Pay? District Magnitude, Ethnic Composition, and the Vote Shares of Moderate Parties."**
Cross-national district-level analysis (CLEA, EPAC) in divided societies, complemented by a sub-national case study of Lebanon's 2017 electoral reform. Finds that higher district magnitude benefits moderate parties specifically in ethnically mixed districts.

**3. "Is More Better? The Effect of Increasing the Number of Votes on Moderate and Cross-Ethnic Voting."**
An original individual-level experiment in Bosnia and Herzegovina (designed, funded, and fielded by the author) testing whether increasing the number of votes per voter encourages cross-ethnic and moderate voting. Finds that increasing to three votes per voter raises support for non-ethnic parties, with an added effect on moderate voting.

**4. "Affective Polarization: Disentangling Policy and Identity"** (with Toine Fiselier and Filip Kostelka) — survey experiment, in preparation for submission.
